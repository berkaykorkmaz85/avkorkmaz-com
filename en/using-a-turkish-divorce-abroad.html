Turkish Divorce Certificate: How to Use a Turkish Divorce Abroad (Finality Note, Apostille, Translation – 2026)

You obtained a divorce in Turkey and now need it in another country, for example to remarry, to change your name or to update your marital status. This guide explains which documents you need and in which order you should obtain them.

Short answer

  • A Turkish divorce decision can be used abroad only after it has become final.
  • You need a certified copy of the decision that carries the finality note (kesinleşme şerhi).
  • Most countries also ask for an apostille and a sworn translation.
  • Each country decides whether it registers the divorce directly or requires a recognition procedure.

Step 1: The decision must become final

A Turkish divorce decision does not take effect as soon as the judge announces it. The parties can appeal to the regional court of appeal (istinaf) within two weeks. This period starts separately for each party when the reasoned decision is formally served on (delivered to) that party (Code of Civil Procedure – HMK, Law No. 6100, Art. 345).

If nobody appeals, the decision becomes final when the period expires. If a party appeals, the decision becomes final only when the appeal stages open in the case have been completed (the regional court of appeal and, where available, the Court of Cassation) or when the time limit for a further appeal has expired. In uncontested divorces the parties can waive their right to appeal, but only after the reasoned decision has been served on them (HMK Art. 349); the decision can then become final sooner.

Tip: If your spouse lives abroad, formal service of the decision can take several months. The two-week period does not start for your spouse until service has been completed. The finality note can be issued only when the appeal periods of both spouses have ended or both have validly waived their right to appeal.

Step 2: Certified copy with the finality note

Once the decision is final, the court records this on the decision. The court writes the finality note below or on the back of the decision, adds the date and the court seal, and the judge signs it (Code of Civil Procedure Art. 302/4). Today the note is usually issued with a secure electronic signature through the national judiciary network (UYAP).

In most cases, request a certified copy of the full, reasoned decision with the finality note from the court that issued it; some authorities also accept a separate certificate of finality, so check their list first. A short summary of the judgment or a screenshot from the court system is not enough for foreign authorities.

The court also sends the final decision to the Turkish civil registry. If one spouse is a Turkish citizen, the divorce is then recorded in that spouse's entry in the Turkish population register.

Step 3: Apostille

Turkey is a party to the Hague Apostille Convention of 1961. Documents issued by Turkish courts therefore usually need an apostille to be accepted in another member country. For court documents, the apostille is generally issued by the Justice Commission of the first-instance courts (Adli Yargı İlk Derece Mahkemesi Adalet Komisyonu Başkanlığı) at the courthouse. Confirm the competent office with the court that issued the decision before you apply.

If the country where you will use the decision is not a party to the Apostille Convention, the document needs legalisation instead. In that case the Turkish authorities first certify the document, and then the consulate of that country in Turkey legalises it.

Step 4: Sworn translation

Foreign authorities usually require a translation by a sworn translator. Have the decision, the finality note and the apostille translated together. Some authorities accept a translation made in Turkey and certified by a Turkish notary; others require a translator registered in their own country. Ask the receiving authority which version it accepts before you order the translation.

Step 5: Registration or recognition abroad

Every country applies its own rules to foreign divorces. In some countries the civil registry records the Turkish decision directly when you present the documents above. Other countries require a separate recognition decision from a court or a ministry before the divorce takes effect there.

In Germany, for example, a foreign divorce generally needs recognition by the state justice administration (Landesjustizverwaltung) under § 107 of the Family Procedure Act (FamFG). This step is not required if both spouses were Turkish citizens at the time of the decision. Contact the competent authority in your country to learn which procedure applies to you.

The reverse situation is covered in our guide to recognition of a foreign divorce in Turkey.

Other documents you may need

Many foreign authorities also ask for a civil registry extract that shows the divorce (vukuatlı nüfus kayıt örneği). This is an administrative document, not a court document. Its apostille is therefore issued by the district governorship or the governorship (kaymakamlık or valilik), not by the Justice Commission at the courthouse.

Remarrying after a Turkish divorce

If you want to remarry in Turkey, note that Turkish law sets a waiting period for women: a woman may not remarry until 300 days have passed since the end of the marriage. The period ends with the birth of a child, and the court lifts it if the woman is shown not to be pregnant or if the former spouses want to remarry each other (Turkish Civil Code Art. 132). If you want to remarry in another country, that country’s rules decide whether and when you can marry; it will usually ask for the final Turkish decision with the apostille and translation described above.

Can you do this without travelling to Turkey?

You do not need to come to Turkey to collect these documents. A lawyer in Turkey can request the certified decision with the finality note, apply for the apostille and arrange the sworn translation with your power of attorney. You can give the power of attorney at a Turkish consulate or before a notary in your country with an apostille.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does a Turkish divorce become final?

The decision becomes final when the two-week appeal period expires without an appeal, or when the appeal stages open in the case have been completed. The period starts when the decision is formally served on each party (Code of Civil Procedure Art. 345).

What is the finality note?

The finality note (kesinleşme şerhi) states that the decision has become final. The court writes it on the decision with the date, the court seal and the judge's signature (Code of Civil Procedure Art. 302/4).

Where do I get an apostille for a Turkish court decision?

Court documents usually receive their apostille from the Justice Commission of the first-instance courts (Adli Yargı İlk Derece Mahkemesi Adalet Komisyonu Başkanlığı) at the courthouse. Confirm the competent office with the court that issued the decision.

Does the other country recognise my Turkish divorce automatically?

It depends on that country. Some countries register a foreign divorce directly, while others require a separate recognition procedure. Ask the civil registry or the competent authority there before you submit the documents.

Can a lawyer obtain these documents for me from abroad?

Yes. A lawyer in Turkey can request the certified decision with the finality note, the apostille and the sworn translation with your power of attorney, so you do not need to travel.

How long does it take to get the finality note?

It depends on how quickly the decision is served and on whether anyone appeals. If both spouses are in Turkey and nobody appeals, the note can usually be requested a few weeks after service; service abroad or an appeal can extend this considerably.

Can I remarry abroad before my home country registers the Turkish divorce?

That depends on the law of the country where you want to marry. Many countries require the divorce to be registered or recognised there first, so ask the marriage registry before you set a date.
