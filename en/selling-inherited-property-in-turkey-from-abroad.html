Selling Inherited Property in Turkey from Abroad: A Guide for Heirs (2026)

A practical guide for heirs living outside Turkey: how inherited real estate is held, how it can be sold, what to do if another heir refuses, and how to handle the process without travelling.

Att. Berkay Korkmaz — Denizli Bar Association (Reg. No: 3530)Updated: 17 November 2026

Your parent or grandparent has passed away and left a house, flat or plot of land in Turkey. You live abroad, your siblings or cousins live in Turkey, and nobody agrees on what to do with the property. Can you sell it? Can you force a sale? Do you have to travel to Turkey? This guide explains the legal steps under Turkish law.

Short answer

  • Until the estate is divided, all heirs own the property together and must act together (Turkish Civil Code, Art. 640).
  • If all heirs agree, the property can be sold at the land registry; heirs abroad can act through a power of attorney.
  • If one heir refuses, any heir can ask the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) to end the co-ownership. If the property cannot be divided, the court orders a public auction (Civil Code Art. 642, 699).
  • Before filing that case, you must first apply to a mediator (Law No. 6325, Art. 18/B).
  • You do not need to travel to Turkey for most of these steps.

Step 1: Certificate of inheritance

Everything starts with the certificate of inheritance (mirasçılık belgesi or veraset ilamı). It shows who the legal heirs are and their shares. A notary or a civil court of peace in Turkey issues it on application (Civil Code Art. 598). A lawyer acting under your power of attorney can obtain it for you. A foreign certificate of inheritance (for example a German Erbschein) is generally not enough: the Turkish land registry asks for a Turkish certificate or for a foreign decision recognised in Turkey.

Why one heir cannot simply sell “their share”

After death, the heirs form a community of heirs. They own the estate jointly (elbirliği mülkiyeti) and have no separate, defined shares in each asset; each heir’s right extends to the whole property (Civil Code Art. 640, 701).

The Turkish Court of Cassation has confirmed that a buyer cannot be registered as owner of an heir’s share while the joint ownership continues; the joint ownership must first be converted into ordinary co-ownership with defined shares (paylı mülkiyet) (Court of Cassation, 7th Civil Chamber, 2021/6443 E., 2023/796 K., 13.02.2023).

In practice, there are three ways forward:

  1. All heirs agree to sell the whole property to a buyer.
  2. The heirs convert the joint ownership into defined shares; then each heir can sell their own share.
  3. If there is no agreement, an heir files a case to end the co-ownership (ortaklığın giderilmesi).

If you sell your share: the other heirs’ right of pre-emption

Once you own a defined share and sell it to an outsider, the other co-owners can use their right of pre-emption (önalım hakkı) and take the share over from the buyer (Civil Code Art. 732). The sale must be notified to them through a notary. Their right expires three months after notification and in any case one year after the sale (Art. 733).

Since an amendment of 24 December 2025 (Law No. 7571), a co-owner who uses this right must deposit the market value of the share as determined by the judge (Art. 734). Buyers often know about this risk, which can affect the price you are offered for a share.

The Constitutional Court has annulled the words “market value” in this rule (decision of 16 July 2026, E.2026/23, K.2026/162, published in the Official Gazette on 6 November 2026). The annulment takes effect nine months after publication, on 6 August 2027. Until then the market-value rule still applies, and Parliament may adopt a new rule in the meantime. Ask for the current position before you sell.

Note that the right of pre-emption applies to shares in co-ownership (paylı mülkiyet). While the estate is still held jointly, an heir can only sign a notarised contract with a third party about their inheritance share. That contract does not make the third party an owner; it only gives a claim to whatever the heir receives when the estate is divided (Civil Code Art. 677).

If an heir refuses: the case to end co-ownership

Every heir may ask for the estate to be divided at any time, unless they are bound to stay in co-ownership by contract or by law (Civil Code Art. 642). The court first considers dividing the property in kind. If physical division is not suitable, especially if the property would lose significant value, the court orders a sale by public auction and the proceeds are shared according to the heirs’ shares (Art. 699). Today such auctions are usually held online through the judiciary’s electronic auction portal (e-Satış).

  • Mediation first: Since 2023, disputes about ending co-ownership require an application to a mediator before a case can be filed (Law No. 6325, Art. 18/B). If the heirs reach an agreement in mediation, the agreement becomes enforceable once the court issues an enforceability annotation (icra edilebilirlik şerhi).
  • Competent court: The civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) hears the case.
  • Duration and costs: They depend on the number of heirs, valuation by experts and the court’s workload. No fixed timeline can be guaranteed.

Handling everything from abroad: power of attorney

You can authorise a lawyer in Turkey with a power of attorney. There are two common ways:

  • at a Turkish consulate in your country, which issues the document in Turkish; or
  • before a local notary, with an apostille and a sworn Turkish translation.

Make sure the power of attorney expressly covers the steps you need, for example obtaining the certificate of inheritance, mediation, filing the case, and selling or buying real estate at the land registry. A general document may not be accepted for every step.

At a Turkish consulate you usually need an appointment, which you can book online at konsolosluk.gov.tr, and you must attend in person. A power of attorney that covers a land registry sale is drawn up by the consulate in the official notarial form (düzenleme şeklinde). If your country is not a party to the Apostille Convention, a document signed before a local notary needs consular legalisation instead of an apostille.

Inheritance tax declaration in Turkey

Heirs must file an inheritance tax return in Turkey. If the death occurred in Turkey and the heir lives abroad, the deadline is six months from the date of death. If the death occurred abroad, the deadline is four, six or eight months depending on where the heirs live (Inheritance and Transfer Tax Law No. 7338, Art. 9). Your home country may also tax the inheritance; ask a tax adviser there.

For readers in the UK, Ireland and the US

Documents issued in these countries usually need an apostille from the competent authority before they can be used in Turkey. Death certificates, birth certificates and powers of attorney prepared abroad also need a sworn Turkish translation. Check the requirements with the Turkish consulate before you sign anything.

Checklist

  • Collect the death certificate and your identity documents.
  • Find out the property’s land registry details (province, district, parcel).
  • Ask the other heirs in writing whether they agree to sell.
  • Sign a power of attorney at the consulate or before a notary with an apostille.
  • Note the inheritance tax deadline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell my inherited share in Turkey without my siblings?

You cannot do so while the estate is held jointly. You can either agree with all heirs to sell, convert the joint ownership into defined shares, or file a case to end the co-ownership (Civil Code Art. 640, 642).

Can my siblings stop the sale?

They can refuse a voluntary sale, but they cannot prevent a court from ending the co-ownership. If the property cannot be divided, the court orders a public auction (Civil Code Art. 699).

Do I have to come to Turkey?

In most cases you do not need to. A lawyer can act for you with a power of attorney issued at a Turkish consulate or before a notary with an apostille.

Is mediation compulsory?

Yes. Before filing a case to end co-ownership, an application to a mediator is required (Law No. 6325, Art. 18/B).

What is the deadline for the inheritance tax return?

If the death occurred in Turkey and you live abroad, the deadline is six months from the date of death (Law No. 7338, Art. 9).

Can we communicate in English?

Yes, in writing by e-mail or WhatsApp. Where a call is needed, we can arrange an interpreter.
